Why this matters

Nobody tells you the rules until after they decline you.

Funding decisions run on a short list of factors that are almost never explained to the applicant. The result is a pattern that repeats across thousands of small businesses.

Personal cards become the default

Inventory, payroll gaps, equipment, and ad spend land on personal credit. The interest is high, the balance follows you personally, and every dollar of it counts against your personal profile.

Rejections stack up quietly

Applying without preparation produces inquiries and declines. Several rushed applications in a short window can make the next approval harder than the first one would have been.

The business has no profile of its own

An entity with no EIN-linked history, no business bank account, and no reported accounts is invisible to underwriting. Everything gets judged on you personally instead.

The fix is ordinary: know what gets reviewed, prepare the profile deliberately, and apply in a sequence that makes sense. That is what the checklist walks you through.

The education

How business funding actually works

Three different instruments, three different bars to clear. Most owners only ever hear about one of them. Which one fits depends on your documentation and how you plan to use the money.

1
Usually the entry point

Business credit cards

Revolving accounts opened in the business name and underwritten primarily on your personal credit profile, with a personal guarantee. Many carry introductory 0% APR periods, which is why they are often the first tool owners use for inventory, ad spend, or equipment. Approval leans on your personal profile far more than on business revenue, so this path is open to newer entities.

Typically needsEIN, entity, strong personal profile
DocumentationLight — often stated revenue
Best forShort-cycle, repayable spend
2
Requires paperwork

Business lines of credit

A revolving limit you draw against and repay, priced as interest rather than a card APR. This is the step up — and the reason it is a step up is documentation. Lenders want to see the business's own financial history: bank statements, tax returns, and time in business. If you have the records, a line of credit is usually cheaper and more flexible than card debt for working capital.

Typically needsBank statements, tax returns, time in business
DocumentationHeavier — full financial file
Best forWorking capital and cash-flow gaps
3
When it makes sense

Personal-backed options

Personal credit lines and personal loans deployed into the business. Sometimes this is the right call — a brand-new entity with no history may have no other realistic path to starting capital. But it is a real trade: the obligation sits on you personally and consumes personal capacity you may want later. It should be a deliberate choice, not a fallback you land on.

Typically needsPersonal profile and income
DocumentationPersonal, not business
Trade-offPersonal liability and capacity

What underwriting looks at

Six factors decide what you qualify for

These are the levers. Some you can move in a few weeks, some take longer — but every one of them is knowable before you submit a single application.

1. Entity and EIN setup

A registered entity, an EIN, a business bank account, and consistent naming and address details across every record.

2. Personal credit profile

Score band, payment history, and derogatory marks. On card products this carries the most weight of anything on the list.

3. Credit utilization

How much of your existing revolving credit is already in use. High utilization suppresses both approvals and limits.

4. Time in business

How long the entity has existed and been operating. It gates which products are realistically available to you.

5. Revenue and deposits

Consistency of monthly deposits in the business account. This is what a line of credit is sized against.

6. Recent inquiries

How many applications you have submitted lately, and how they were spaced. Sequence and timing genuinely matter.
