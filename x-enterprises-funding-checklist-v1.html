Readiness Checklist

What card issuers and lenders review before they approve you — and the gaps to close first. Work through this before you submit a single application.

How to use this Print it. Check off what you have. Anything unchecked is your work list.

Funding is not a lottery. It runs on a short list of factors, and every one of them is knowable before you apply. The owners who get better outcomes are almost never the ones with a secret — they are the ones who prepared the file first and applied in a sensible order.

1. The document pack

Gather these before you start anything. Being asked for a document you do not have is the most common reason an application stalls out.

  • Formation documents Articles of organization or incorporation, plus your operating agreement or bylaws.
  • EIN confirmation letter The IRS CP 575 notice, or a 147C letter if you have misplaced the original.
  • Business bank statements — last 3 to 6 months From an account in the business name. Personal statements do not substitute for these.
  • Business tax returns — last 1 to 2 years Only if the business has filed. A newer entity is not disqualified for lacking these.
  • Personal tax returns — last 1 to 2 years Frequently requested, since most business credit carries a personal guarantee.
  • Government-issued photo ID Name and address should match your other records exactly.
  • Voided business check or bank letter Used to verify the account funding would be deposited into.
  • A current profit and loss statement Even a simple one. It shows you know your own numbers, which matters more than its formatting.

2. Entity and EIN readiness

Underwriting cross-references your details across several sources. Mismatches read as risk, and they are entirely avoidable.

  • The entity is registered and in good standing Check your Secretary of State listing. An administratively dissolved entity is an immediate stop.
  • You have an EIN tied to that entity Not your SSN. Free directly from the IRS — never pay a third party for one.
  • A dedicated business bank account exists and is in use Running business income through a personal account undercuts everything else on this list.
  • Business name, address, and phone match everywhere Entity filing, EIN letter, bank account, website, invoices. "Suite 4" and "Ste 4" can differ enough to matter.
  • The business address is not a residence or a PO box, where avoidable Not always disqualifying, but a commercial or registered agent address is cleaner.
  • The business is reachable A working phone number and a live website. Some issuers do verify that both exist.
  • Your industry classification is accurate Certain codes are restricted by certain issuers. Knowing yours in advance saves a wasted application.

3. Credit profile prep

On the products most owners start with, your personal profile carries more weight than anything about the business. These are in the order worth addressing them.

  • Pull all three of your personal reports Free at annualcreditreport.com — the federally authorized source. Checking your own does not affect your score.
  • Verify every account listed is actually yours Errors are common. Anything genuinely inaccurate can be corrected directly with the bureau at no cost.
  • Bring revolving utilization down The single fastest-moving factor on this list. Lower reported balances generally help both approvals and limits.
  • Confirm no late payments in recent months Recency weighs heavily. A clean recent stretch matters more than a spotless distant past.
  • Count your inquiries from the last six months A cluster of recent applications tells underwriting you have been shopping hard and getting declined.
  • Do not close your oldest account to "clean up" Account age helps you. Closing an old card removes that history and can raise your utilization.
  • Know your actual score before you apply Applying blind is how people find out their band the expensive way.

4. Match the funding type to the use of money

The right instrument depends on what the money is for and how quickly it comes back. A mismatch here is how affordable funding turns into expensive funding.

Funding type Typically needs Fits best Watch for
Business credit cards Entity, EIN, strong personal profile. Light documentation. Inventory, ad spend, equipment — spend that recycles quickly. What the rate becomes after any introductory period ends.
Business line of credit Bank statements, tax returns, time in business, revenue history. Working capital and cash-flow gaps you draw against and repay. Draw fees, maintenance fees, and renewal terms.
Personal-backed options Your personal profile and income. No business history required. Starting capital when the business has no file of its own yet. The obligation is yours personally, and it consumes personal capacity.

5. Six reasons applications get declined

Every one of these is preventable with preparation.

Applying to everything at once

A burst of applications in a short window reads as distress and stacks inquiries against you.

Fix: Decide on a sequence first, and space applications deliberately.

High utilization at the moment of review

Your profile is judged on the day it is pulled, not on your intentions for next month.

Fix: Bring balances down and let them report before you apply.

Mismatched business details

Name, address, or phone that differ between your filing, your bank, and your application.

Fix: Pick one exact format and correct every record to match it.

No business bank account

Business income running through a personal account leaves the entity with no financial history.

Fix: Open one, route all business income through it, and let it season.

Documents you cannot produce

An underwriter asks for statements or returns, and the file goes quiet while you hunt for them.

Fix: Assemble section 1 of this checklist before you start.

Asking for an amount the file cannot support

A request far beyond what your revenue and profile justify gets declined outright rather than reduced.

Fix: Size the request to what your documentation actually supports.

6. Questions to ask before you accept anything

Ask these of any funding offer, from anyone — including us. A straight answer to each one is the minimum bar.

  1. What is the total cost of this money, in dollars, over the full term? Not the rate. The dollar amount you will have paid when it is finished.
  2. Is this a fixed rate or an introductory rate, and what does it become afterward?
  3. What fees apply — origination, draw, maintenance, annual, prepayment?
  4. Is there a personal guarantee, and what exactly does it obligate me to?
  5. Does this report to business credit, personal credit, or both? This determines whether it builds a business profile or consumes your personal capacity.
  6. What happens if I repay it early — do I save interest, or is it penalized?
  7. Who is the actual issuer, and what is the consultant or broker being paid?
