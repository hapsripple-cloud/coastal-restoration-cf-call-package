Business Funding
Readiness Checklist

What card issuers and lenders review before they decide, and the gaps worth closing first. Work through this before you submit a single application.

How to use this

Print it. Check off what you already have. Whatever stays unchecked is your work list, roughly in priority order.

01

The document pack

Assemble these before you start anything. Being asked for a document you cannot produce is the most common reason a file stalls halfway through review.

  • Formation documents Articles of organization or incorporation, plus your operating agreement or bylaws.
  • EIN confirmation letter The IRS CP 575 notice, or a 147C letter if the original has gone missing.
  • Business bank statements, last 3 to 6 months From an account in the business name. Personal statements do not substitute for these.
  • Business tax returns, last 1 to 2 years Only if the business has filed. A newer entity is not disqualified for lacking them.
  • Personal tax returns, last 1 to 2 years Frequently requested, since most business credit carries a personal guarantee.
  • Government-issued photo ID Name and address should match your other records exactly.
  • Voided business check or bank letter Verifies the account any funding would be deposited into.
  • A current profit and loss statement Even a simple one. It shows you know your own numbers, which counts for more than its formatting.
02

Entity and EIN readiness

Underwriting cross-references your details across several sources. Mismatches read as risk, and every one of them is avoidable.

  • The entity is registered and in good standing Check your Secretary of State listing. An administratively dissolved entity is an immediate stop.
  • You have an EIN tied to that entity Not your SSN. Free directly from the IRS, and never worth paying a third party for.
  • A dedicated business bank account exists and is in use Running business income through a personal account undercuts everything else on this list.
  • Business name, address, and phone match everywhere Entity filing, EIN letter, bank account, website, invoices. "Suite 4" and "Ste 4" can differ enough to matter.
  • The business address is not a residence or PO box, where avoidable Not always disqualifying, but a commercial or registered agent address reads cleaner.
  • The business is reachable A working phone number and a live website. Some issuers do verify both exist.
  • Your industry classification is accurate Some codes are restricted by some issuers. Knowing yours in advance saves a wasted application.
03

Credit profile prep

On the products most owners start with, your personal profile carries more weight than anything about the business. Listed in the order worth working through them.

  • Pull all three of your personal reports Free at annualcreditreport.com, the federally authorized source. Checking your own has no effect on your score.
  • Verify every account listed is actually yours Errors are common. Anything genuinely inaccurate can be corrected with the bureau directly at no cost.
  • Bring revolving utilization down The fastest-moving factor here. Lower reported balances generally help both approvals and limits.
  • Confirm no late payments in recent months Recency weighs heavily. A clean recent stretch counts for more than a spotless distant past.
  • Count your inquiries from the last six months A cluster of recent applications tells underwriting you have been shopping hard and getting declined.
  • Do not close your oldest account to tidy up Account age works in your favor. Closing an old card removes that history and can raise your utilization.
  • Know your actual score before you apply Applying blind is how people learn their band the expensive way.
04

Match the instrument to the money

The right instrument depends on what the money is for and how fast it comes back. A mismatch here is how affordable funding turns expensive.

Instrument Typically needs Fits Watch for
Business credit cards Entity, EIN, strong personal profile. Light documentation. Inventory, ad spend, equipment. Spend that recycles quickly. What the rate becomes after any introductory period ends.
Business line of credit Bank statements, tax returns, time in business, revenue history. Working capital and cash-flow gaps you draw against and repay. Draw fees, maintenance fees, and renewal terms.
Personal-backed Your personal profile and income. No business history required. Starting capital when the business has no file of its own yet. The obligation is yours, and it consumes personal capacity.
05

Why applications get declined

Six patterns account for most of it, and every one is preventable with preparation.

  • Applying to everything at once

    A burst of applications in a short window reads as distress and stacks inquiries against you.

    Fix  Decide the sequence first, then space the applications deliberately.

  • High utilization on the day of review

    Your file is judged when it is pulled, not on what you intend to pay down next month.

    Fix  Bring balances down and let them report before you apply.

  • Mismatched business details

    Name, address, or phone that differ between your filing, your bank, and your application.

    Fix  Pick one exact format and correct every record to match it.

  • No business bank account

    Business income running through a personal account leaves the entity with no financial history to read.

    Fix  Open one, route all business income through it, and let it season.

  • Documents you cannot produce

    An underwriter asks for statements or returns, and the file goes quiet while you hunt for them.

    Fix  Assemble section 01 before you start.

  • Asking for more than the file supports

    A request far beyond what your revenue and profile justify gets declined outright rather than reduced.

    Fix  Size the request to what your documentation actually carries.

06

Questions to ask before you accept

Ask these of any funding offer, from anyone, including us. A straight answer to each is the minimum bar.

  1. What is the total cost of this money, in dollars, over the full term? Not the rate. The dollar figure you will have paid when it is finished.
  2. Is this a fixed rate or an introductory rate, and what does it become after?
  3. What fees apply: origination, draw, maintenance, annual, prepayment?
  4. Is there a personal guarantee, and what exactly does it obligate me to?
  5. Does this report to business credit, personal credit, or both? This decides whether it builds a business profile or consumes your personal capacity.
  6. What happens if I repay early: do I save interest, or is it penalized?
  7. Who is the actual issuer, and what is the consultant or broker being paid?
